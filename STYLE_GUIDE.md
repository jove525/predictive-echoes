# PredictiveEchoes — Style Guide

> This is fed to Claude during script generation to enforce consistent voice and format.

---

## Voice & Tone
- Authoritative but not academic — no jargon without immediate explanation
- Conversational — write how a smart person TALKS, not how they write essays
- Provocative — every paragraph should make the viewer want to keep watching
- Debate-inviting — plant questions, present both sides, don't moralize
- Never partisan — analyze incentives and patterns, not ideology

## Sentence Construction
- Short sentences. Under 20 words preferred.
- Use rhetorical questions to maintain tension. "Sound familiar?"
- Vary rhythm — short. Then a slightly longer sentence that builds. Then short again.
- Avoid passive voice entirely
- Use "here's the trap" / "here's the question" / "here's the pattern" as structural beats

## Script Length
- Target: 1,200–1,600 words (approx. 8–11 minutes at natural speaking pace)
- Minimum: 1,000 words
- Maximum: 1,800 words

## Mandatory Structure
```
[HOOK]         ~80 words    — current event + "most analysts are missing this"
[INTRO]        ~100 words   — introduce thinker, why this lecture matters
[CORE THESIS]  ~200 words   — the big idea, stripped of jargon
[QUOTE/CLIP]   ~50 words    — direct quote from source (transcript excerpt)
[HISTORICAL 1] ~150 words   — first parallel, labeled "First,"
[HISTORICAL 2] ~150 words   — second parallel, labeled "Second,"
[HISTORICAL 3] ~150 words   — third parallel, labeled "Third,"
[PREDICTIONS]  ~300 words   — 5 numbered predictions, specific + time-bound
[SYNTHESIS]    ~150 words   — tie it together, restate the pattern
[CTA]          ~80 words    — one debate question + subscribe ask
```

## Forbidden Phrases
- "In conclusion" / "In summary" — too academic
- "It's important to note" — filler
- "As we can see" — condescending
- "In today's complex world" — cliché opener
- Any phrase that sounds like an essay introduction

## Citations & Attribution
- Always credit Prof. Jiang or source thinker by full name on first mention
- "Professor Jiang argues..." / "According to Professor Jiang's framework..."
- Always mention the original lecture in the CTA and description
- Position the channel as a commentator/explainer, not an original researcher

## Title Formula (for YouTube)
Pattern: `[Thinker]'s [Year] [Warning/Prediction/Theory]: [Bold Claim] — [Evidence/Method]`
Examples:
- "Prof. Jiang's 2026 Warning: US Empire Collapse Begins – Game Theory Proof"
- "Ray Dalio's Debt Cycle Theory: Why 2026 Is the Breaking Point"
- "Peter Zeihan's Resource War Prediction: China's Energy Trap Explained"

## Description Template
```
[2–3 sentence hook from the video]

In this video, we break down [Thinker]'s [thesis] — [why it matters now].

🔗 Original lecture: [LINK]
📚 Sources mentioned: [LIST]

Subscribe for weekly deep dives into geopolitical patterns, empire cycles, and predictive history.

#Geopolitics #EmpireCycles #PredictiveHistory #[Thinker] #2026
```

## Thumbnail Style
- Dark/dramatic background (maps, cityscapes, war imagery — stock)
- Bold white + red/yellow text
- Max 6 words on thumbnail
- Thinker's name or face if fair use allows
- Question or warning format: "EMPIRE COLLAPSE 2026?" or "PROF. JIANG WAS RIGHT"
